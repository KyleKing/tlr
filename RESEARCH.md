# Linear in 2026: what's native, what's still missing, and what it means for tlr

Research compiled September 2026. Two warnings up front. First, a large share of the search surface for "Linear 2026" is content-farm output: [shipped.news](https://www.shipped.news/articles/linear-cycles-2-auto-scheduling.html) carries a detailed "Cycles 2.0" launch story dated May 29, 2026 with a quote from a "Kareem Hassan, Linear Head of Engineering" and claims of AI capacity estimation with PTO-aware calendar integration. None of it appears in [Linear's own changelog](https://linear.app/changelog) or [Cycles docs](https://linear.app/docs/use-cycles), and the article cites no sources. Treat it as fabricated. The same pattern shows up on workmanagementhub.com, aiproductivity.ai, exceeds.ai, codepulsehq.com, and coderbuds.com. Everything below is anchored to vendor docs, changelogs, pricing pages, or HN threads where possible. Second, some claims I could not verify are marked as such.

## 1. What Linear now offers natively

**Insights and Dashboards.** [Insights](https://linear.app/insights) measures issue count, effort, cycle time, triage time, lead time, and issue age, sliced by status, assignee, creator, priority, labels, SLA status, estimates, templates, projects, initiatives, teams, and date fields. Charts are lines, bars, and tables, grouped daily through yearly. Export goes to CSV, Google Sheets, Fivetran, and Airbyte. Per [Linear's pricing page](https://linear.app/pricing), Insights and Dashboards both sit on Business ($16/user/mo annual) and Enterprise. There is no capacity, no per-person load, and no scenario modeling in Insights.

**Cycles and capacity.** [Cycles docs](https://linear.app/docs/use-cycles) describe a capacity dial: "Capacity is calculated from the velocity of the previous three completed cycles, i.e. the number of issues or estimate points completed in these cycles," falling back to a headcount-based guess for new teams. This is a team-level number. It is not per-person, and nothing deflates it for on-call or time off. Carry-over is automatic: "Open issues generally roll over automatically, but issues moved to backlog, triage, canceled, or completed during cooldown are not carried into the next cycle." Completed cycle graphs are frozen snapshots, and the docs warn those snapshots can diverge from the issues' current state.

**Project forecasting is real and it overlaps tlr.** The [project graph](https://linear.app/docs/project-graph) shows an estimated completion date "once the project has enough history to calculate a weekly velocity," weighting recent weeks more heavily, drawn as a dotted projection with optimistic and pessimistic variants at roughly ±40%. That is a velocity-based slip forecast at project level, shipped.

**Project and initiative updates, health.** Health (On track / At risk / Off track) is [chosen by a human](https://linear.app/docs/initiative-and-project-updates), not computed. Linear auto-generates the progress section (delays, target-date changes, new leads, milestone progress) and admins can set update cadence with reminders. Since [June 18, 2026](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-06-18-agent-assisted-project-updates), a "Write with Agent" button drafts the update from recent issue changes, linked documents, and the linked Slack channel.

**Initiatives.** [Team initiatives](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-08-13-team-initiatives) (Aug 13, 2026) assign a team as initiative lead. [Initiative properties](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-07-02-initiative-properties) (Jul 2, 2026) added statuses, priorities, and labels. Sub-initiatives are Business-tier.

**Triage, duplicates, SLAs.** [Triage Intelligence](https://linear.app/docs/triage-intelligence) (Business/Enterprise) suggests team, assignee, labels, and project, with reasoning, and flags "strong semantic similarity between an issue in Triage and an existing issue" as a duplicate relation. Labels can auto-apply. Underneath is the embedding-based [Similar Issues](https://linear.app/now/using-ai-to-detect-similar-issues) work Linear described publicly. Issue SLAs are Business/Enterprise per the pricing page. [Linear Asks](https://linear.app/docs/linear-asks) turns Slack, email, and (Enterprise) web-form requests into issues.

**Agents.** [Linear for Agents](https://linear.app/agents) defines an Agent Session lifecycle: sessions are created when an agent is mentioned or delegated an issue, and state is visible to users, with [webhook-driven events](https://linear.app/developers/agents). 2026 shipped [Linear Diffs](https://linear.app/changelog) native code review (May 28), [coding sessions using Claude](https://linear.app/changelog) (Jun 11), [Loops](https://linear.app/changelog) recurring agent tasks on schedule or event triggers (Jul 20, Business/Enterprise, AI credits), [Guided Reviews GA](https://linear.app/changelog) and [GitHub Copilot for Linear](https://linear.app/changelog) (Jul 30), and [text attribution with agent-assisted document editing](https://linear.app/changelog) (Jul 23) that highlights agent changes and shows who wrote what.

**API.** `IssueHistory` entries carry `fromCycle`/`toCycle` alongside `fromAssignee`/`toAssignee` and `fromState`/`toState` ([schema reference](https://studio.apollographql.com/public/Linear-API/variant/current/schema/reference/objects/IssueHistory), [Linear GraphQL docs](https://linear.app/developers/graphql)). Cross-cycle carry-over is reconstructible from the API without local snapshots.

## 2. Gaps people still complain about

Linear's CEO has said outright that the company tries hard to ignore manager-reporting requests, because they add administrative burden to the people doing the work ([Siit review](https://www.siit.io/tools/trending/linear-app-review) citing that position). That is a structural gap, not a backlog item.

Concretely, reviewers and users report: reporting that is underpowered past basic views, no issue-level Gantt or timeline (timeline is project-only, so no issue-by-issue scheduling), no native release or fix-version management, and limited dashboard building ([Siit](https://www.siit.io/tools/trending/linear-app-review), [G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/linear/reviews)). Dependencies exist as issue relations (blocking / blocked by / related / duplicate) shown in the sidebar ([docs](https://linear.app/docs/issue-relations)) and as project-level end→start links on the timeline ([docs](https://linear.app/docs/project-dependencies)), but there is no chain-risk view: nothing rolls a blocked-by graph up into "this milestone is at risk because of a three-deep chain."

The [Issue Tracking Is Dead HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47507253) is the sharpest recent signal on sentiment. Representative: "I want it to stay far away from my issue tracker. To me that's a sacred space that can't be polluted by LLM noise." Others argue agents will sidestep trackers entirely since feedback originates in Slack, calls, and Twitter, and worry that auto-generated tickets and PRs arrive without quality oversight. Linear's response in-thread was that usage is growing and agentic workflows reflect real customer behavior.

On calendar-aware planning, nothing native connects Linear cycles to out-of-office. Linear has a [Float integration](https://linear.app/integrations/float) for resource scheduling, which is the tell that Linear does not intend to own capacity itself.

## 3. How tech leads and EMs use AI in 2026

The dominant pattern is meeting-to-ticket: [Spinach](https://www.spinach.ai/blog/ai-tools-for-engineers) joins standups and planning, transcribes, extracts action items, and suggests ticket updates in Jira, Linear, or Asana. [Dailybot](https://www.dailybot.com/solutions/engineering-managers/) does async standups with AI summaries. The recurring vendor claim is that managers spend 7-9 hours a week on status updates ([MintMCP](https://www.mintmcp.com/blog/ai-coworkers-engineering-teams)) — vendor-sourced, unverified, but directionally consistent with why Linear shipped agent-drafted project updates.

The AI-slop and agent-review topic is now well documented. [The New Stack argues every engineering org needs an "AI slop registry"](https://thenewstack.io/engineering-ai-slop-registry/). [Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/08/ai-slop-in-the-enterprise-what-happens-when-engineers-stop-reviewing-ai-generated-code/) reports AI-generated PRs wait roughly 4.6x longer for review with higher rejection rates, and median review duration rising over 440% as AI tooling spread, with unreviewed code merging by default. There is a 2026 paper, "AI Slop and the Software Commons," analyzing 1,100+ Reddit and HN posts (cited via that coverage; I did not locate the primary paper). The framing that matters for tlr: with agent-authored work, the reviewer is often "the first human being to ever lay eyes on this," and nobody has reconstructed intent. Linear's [text attribution](https://linear.app/changelog) feature is its answer for documents.

On tooling shape, the 2026 consensus favors CLI over MCP for agent-driven work. [Arize's eval](https://arize.com/blog/mcp-vs-cli-skills-for-agents-what-our-eval-found-and-which-you-should-use/) found the CLI path executed faster and consumed 90,000 fewer tokens than MCP for the same task; the recommended split is CLI for terminal workflows, MCP for OAuth-gated multi-tenant chat access ([systemprompt.io](https://systemprompt.io/guides/mcp-vs-cli-tools)). This directly validates tlr's spike-then-productionize rule.

## 5. Standup and sprint-lead practices worth encoding

[Mountain Goat Software on spillover](https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/blog/unfinished-work-every-sprint-three-ways-to-break-the-habit) treats repeated unfinished work as a habit with structural causes rather than an estimation error, and prescribes finishing before starting. [Scrum.org's thread on planned vs unplanned carry-over](https://www.scrum.org/forum/scrum-forum/31244/planned-vs-unplanned-carry-over-agile-sprint) distinguishes work planned-but-unfinished from work that arrived mid-sprint; the two need different responses, and conflating them hides the interrupt load. [Plandek's metrics guide](https://plandek.com/blog/the-leader%E2%80%99s-guide-to-agile-metrics) argues repeated spillover signals weak refinement, inflated commitments, undercounted support load, unstable priorities, or quality debt.

On commitment reliability, [Count.co's sprint commitment accuracy metric](https://count.co/metric/sprint-commitment-accuracy) puts high-performing teams at 80-90% of committed work delivered, and frames the percentage as the clearest single signal of planning quality. On WIP, [Scrum.org](https://www.scrum.org/resources/blog/limiting-work-progress-wip-scrum-kanban-what-when-who-how) ties WIP limits to sprint capacity as the mechanism that prevents overcommitment at planning time, and [teachingagile](https://teachingagile.com/kanban/introduction/kanban-wip-limits) covers the per-column mechanics.

Allocation audit against calendar has no canonical source I found. The closest is [Reclaim's engineering positioning](https://reclaim.ai/teams/engineering) (surface where time actually goes) and Jellyfish/Swarmia's investment-balance reporting, both of which audit allocation against intent rather than against calendar availability.

## Implications for tlr

- **Drop duplicate detection.** [Triage Intelligence](https://linear.app/docs/triage-intelligence) does semantic duplicate suggestion at intake on Business/Enterprise, backed by real embedding work. The paused spike in `spike/duplicates/` is competing with a shipped feature that sees issues before they land. Keep it only if you are on a plan below Business.
- **Drop project-level slip forecasting, or narrow it hard.** The [project graph](https://linear.app/docs/project-graph) already draws a velocity-based completion projection with optimistic/pessimistic bands. tlr's forecast only earns its place if it forecasts _milestones within_ a project, or forecasts with capacity deflated for on-call and PTO, which Linear's velocity line cannot do.
- **Keep capacity per person per cycle. This is the crown jewel.** Linear's capacity is a single team number from a three-cycle rolling velocity ([docs](https://linear.app/docs/use-cycles)), with no per-person split and no PTO or on-call deflation. Linear's own answer is to [integrate Float](https://linear.app/integrations/float). Nothing in the changelog contradicts this, and the "AI capacity estimation" stories claiming otherwise are fabricated.
- **Keep the deterministic rebalancing proposal.** No native or adjacent tool proposes per-person reassignment. Linear flags nothing at planning time; Jellyfish and Swarmia report allocation after the fact.
- **Reconsider local snapshots for plan diffs.** `IssueHistory` carries `fromCycle`/`toCycle` plus assignee and state transitions ([schema](https://studio.apollographql.com/public/Linear-API/variant/current/schema/reference/objects/IssueHistory)). Carry-over and re-scoping are reconstructible from the API. Snapshots still earn their place for _estimate_ changes and for things history does not cover, but the overlap is worth auditing before building more on snapshots.
- **Snapshots have a second justification Linear hands you.** The cycle docs warn that completed cycle graphs are frozen while the underlying issues keep changing, so Linear's own history and its charts can disagree. tlr's snapshots can reconcile that.
- **Keep the dependency-chain risk view.** Linear has relations and project-level end→start links ([relations](https://linear.app/docs/issue-relations), [project dependencies](https://linear.app/docs/project-dependencies)) but no transitive chain-risk rollup and no issue-level timeline. Reviewers name the missing issue-level Gantt repeatedly.
- **Keep the AI-slop scan, and sharpen its framing.** This is a live, named industry problem ([AI slop registry](https://thenewstack.io/engineering-ai-slop-registry/), [review-latency data](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/08/ai-slop-in-the-enterprise-what-happens-when-engineers-stop-reviewing-ai-generated-code/)), and the HN thread shows leads actively resent LLM noise in the tracker. Nobody sells a ticket-text slop scanner.
- **Rethink the review queue for bulk AI edits against Linear's attribution work.** Linear now ships [text attribution and agent-edit highlighting](https://linear.app/changelog) for documents, and Agent Sessions make agent activity visible on issues. tlr's queue should key off agent session identity rather than reimplementing "who wrote this."
- **Do not build standup or weekly-update generation.** Linear's [Write with Agent](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-06-18-agent-assisted-project-updates) reads issues, docs, and the linked Slack channel; [Loops](https://linear.app/changelog) runs weekly triage and stalled-task checks on a schedule. Spinach and Dailybot own the meeting side. tlr's differentiator would be feeding capacity truth _into_ an update, not writing the prose.
- **The health signal is the unclaimed one.** Project health is manually chosen and Linear does not compute it. A deterministic "this project's stated health disagrees with its burn-up, its blocked chain, and its owner's remaining capacity" check has no competitor.
- **Consider carry-over classification as a first-class feature.** Splitting planned-but-unfinished from mid-cycle arrivals ([Scrum.org](https://www.scrum.org/forum/scrum-forum/31244/planned-vs-unplanned-carry-over-agile-sprint)) is computable from `fromCycle`/`toCycle` plus issue creation dates, is a recognized practice, and Linear surfaces neither. Same for sprint commitment accuracy against the [80-90% band](https://count.co/metric/sprint-commitment-accuracy).
- **Consider an allocation audit against calendar rather than against intent.** Jellyfish and Swarmia audit allocation against planned investment categories. Nobody audits assigned load against the person's actual available hours after meetings, on-call, and PTO. tlr already has all three inputs.
- **Consider surfacing per-person WIP limits at planning time.** [Scrum.org ties WIP limits to capacity as the anti-overcommitment mechanism](https://www.scrum.org/resources/blog/limiting-work-progress-wip-scrum-kanban-what-when-who-how); Linear has no WIP limit concept at all.
- **The CLI-first bet is the right one and now has evidence.** [Arize's eval](https://arize.com/blog/mcp-vs-cli-skills-for-agents-what-our-eval-found-and-which-you-should-use/) measured a 90,000-token saving for CLI over MCP on equivalent work. Cite it in ADR 0007 rather than arguing the point from first principles.
- **Local-first is a defensible position, not a limitation.** [Clockwise deleted all customer data on a week's notice](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47402579) in March 2026, and [Height shut down](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/3/height-project-management-tool-to-shut-down-by-september-2025/) after $18.3M raised. Say so in the README.
- **Watch the plan-tier line.** Insights, Dashboards, Triage Intelligence, Asks, and SLAs are all [Business ($16/user/mo) or Enterprise](https://linear.app/pricing). For a Basic-tier team, tlr replaces paid Linear features, which changes its pitch to "a Business plan's worth of analysis without the Business plan."
