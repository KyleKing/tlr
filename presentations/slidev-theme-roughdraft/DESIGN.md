# Design System: Roughdraft

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Working Diagram"**

This is a presentation system built to be argued with. Its whole personality comes from
one inversion: a finished-looking slide tells the room the decision is already made, so
this one looks provisional on purpose. The hand-drawn quality is real (every box, rule,
arrow, underline, and circle is generated by roughjs at render time, not drawn as an
asset) but it is applied only to structure. Everything a person has to read sits in a
high-legibility typeface at full ink on a clean cool-white canvas.

The restraint is the design. Excalidraw and tldraw, the pinned references, are quiet
products: generous space, precise interface, wobble confined to the strokes. This theme
follows them there and explicitly rejects the skeuomorphic reading of the same brief.
There is no paper texture, no sticky note, no masking tape, no erase ghosting, and no
infinite-canvas camera work. Those three renditions were built as options and rejected
by name as cheesy, so their absence is a decision, not an omission.

The second constraint shaping everything is the room: cast to a TV in ambient light,
with engineers and non-engineers reading the same slide from different distances. That
makes contrast and minimum type size hard limits rather than preferences, and it is why
a delivery-time high-contrast mode exists as a first-class part of the system.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Wobble in the structure, discipline in the type
- One claim per slide, stated in the headline
- Semantic pen colour, never colour alone
- Deliberate empty space, reserved for live annotation
- Measured legibility over atmosphere, in both palettes

## Colors

A cool near-white canvas carrying two darks and three pens, drawn from Excalidraw's own
diagram palette. That palette works in three layers and this system keeps them separate:
grey-violet outlines, navy text, and pastel fills that are never allowed to become strokes.
Colour is restrained; neutrals own the surface and a pen appears only where it says something.

### Primary

- **Marker Blue** (`#1971c2`, text `#14588f`, fill `#99d5ff`): the caveat and the neutral emphasis. Carries the callout boxes, the diagram's destination node, and the theme's `--slidev-theme-primary`.

### Secondary

- **Correction Red** (`#e03131`, text `#b32424`, fill `#ff9494`): a problem, a correction, or the thing that is broken. The most-used pen after blue, and the one that carries the single deck underline.
- **Affirmative Violet** (`#6965db`, text `#4f4bb8`, fill `#d8d6f5`): the option being recommended, section dividers, and the focus ring. Spent once per slide at most, the way the reference diagram spends it on a single checkmark. Never appears in a chart, so it never competes with data.

### Neutral

- **Cool Canvas** (`#f7f9fa`): every slide surface. Cool rather than cream, because it reads as digital canvas rather than paper.
- **Sunk Panel** (`#ececf4`): code blocks and inline code. The only recessed surface in the system.
- **Letterbox** (`#dfe4e8`): the area around the slide when the display is wider than 16/9. One shade darker than the canvas so the slide edge stays findable on a cast screen.
- **Text Ink** (`#030064`): all body copy and headings. 16.74:1 on canvas. Navy rather than black, which is what keeps the deck from reading as a corporate template.
- **Stroke Ink** (`#46464f`): every shape outline, and secondary text. 8.84:1. Deliberately softer than the text ink, because a stroke dark enough to anchor a diagram is heavier than a diagram needs.
- **Marker Ink** (`#7c7c8a`): list markers and code gutters only. 3.89:1, so never running text.
- **Quiet Pen** (`#8e8e96`, text `#6f6f7d`, fill `#c5c5d0`): structural strokes (rules, frames, chips). 3.08:1, above the non-text threshold. The fill is the faint dashed-connector grey.
- **Code Focus** (`#dae3ec`): the band locating a highlighted group of code lines inside the sunk panel.
- **Code Dim** (`#626a72`): unfocused code lines. Dimming by colour rather than opacity keeps them at 4.67:1 on the panel.

### Named Rules

**The Two Scales Rule.** Every pen has two values. `--rd-pen-{name}` is tuned for a
2px stroke; `--rd-pen-{name}-ink` is the text-safe variant. Text always takes `-ink`.
Breaking this is what put annotation text at 2.42:1 against the canvas, which is the
single most likely way to make this deck unreadable in the room it was built for.

**The Measured Palette Rule.** Every text role in both palettes measures AA or better
against its own background: light palette text ink 16.74:1, stroke ink 8.84:1, the four
`-ink` pens 4.68:1 to 7.05:1; high contrast ink 20.04:1 and pens 8.09:1 to 9.92:1. Any
value below 4.5:1 is a graphic stroke or a decorative marker and is held at or above the
3:1 non-text threshold. A new colour earns its place by measurement, not by taste.

**The Fill-Only Rule.** Every wash is a fill and nothing else. All of them sit under 2:1
on the canvas (`#ff9494` at 2.01:1, `#99d5ff` at 1.49:1), so a wash rendering a stroke or
a glyph is a defect, not a style choice. When a chart needs more categories than there are
pens, the extra ones come from hachure angle and density, never from promoting a wash.

**The Meaning-Not-Decoration Rule.** Red is a problem, blue a caveat, violet the
affirmative. Colour is never the only carrier of a distinction, because a mixed room
includes people who will not see the difference. Green is absent on purpose: it would put
every options comparison on the one axis red-green colour blindness collapses.

## Typography

**Display / Body Font:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next Variable (with `ui-sans-serif`, `system-ui`)
**Annotation Font:** Shantell Sans Variable (with `cursive`)
**Code / Meta Font:** Recursive Variable at `MONO 1`, `CASL 0.32` (with `ui-monospace`)

**Character:** A legibility-first workhorse doing all the reading, with a marker-drawn
face allowed in only to annotate it. Atkinson Hyperlegible was designed for low-vision
readers, which is exactly the TV-across-the-room case; Shantell Sans is tldraw's own
handwriting face, so the annotation voice is literally borrowed from the pinned
reference. Recursive's casual axis lets code sit beside handwriting without imitating it.
All three are self-hosted through `@fontsource`, so a deck presents with no network.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (800, 78px, 1.02, -0.035em): cover and section headlines only.
- **Headline** (800, 50px, 1.08, -0.028em): the `h1` on every content slide. Statement slides run 56px at 1.24 line-height; the closing slide runs 52px.
- **Title** (700, 33px, 1.16): `h2`, used for structural breaks inside a slide.
- **Label** (Shantell, 700, 25px, 1.24): `h3`, `Choice` names, diagram node labels. The label voice.
- **Body** (400, 21px, 1.5, max 65ch): everything read aloud.
- **Note** (Shantell, 500, 19px, 1.42, max 26ch): margin annotations and captions.
- **Meta** (Recursive, 15px, `MONO 1 CASL 0.4`): chips, hints, costs, the slide counter.
- **Code** (Recursive, 17px, 1.62, `MONO 1 CASL 0.32`): code panels.

### Named Rules

**The Annotation Voice Rule.** Shantell Sans is confined to annotation: `Note`, `h3`,
`h4`, blockquotes, `Choice` names, diagram labels. Body copy and `em` emphasis stay in
Atkinson at full ink. Emphasis deliberately does not switch typeface, because switching
mid-sentence breaks the reading line and lightens exactly the words the author wanted
heard.

**The Absolute Pixel Rule.** Type sizes are absolute pixels in Slidev's 980×552 canvas
space, not rem. Slidev scales the whole slide, so relative units buy nothing and make
the ramp harder to reason about.

**The Paired Step Rule.** Every size added to the light palette is added to the `.rd-hc`
block too. Bumping body alone compressed the label-to-body step to 2px in the one mode
that needs hierarchy most.

## Layout

A fixed 980×552 canvas at 16/9, padded 46px vertically and 60px horizontally, scaled by
Slidev to whatever display it lands on. There are no breakpoints and no mobile
composition; the deck is one aspect ratio.

Spacing is a five-step scale (6 / 12 / 20 / 32 / 52px) with more space above a heading
than below it. Body measure is capped at 65ch, two-column text at 38ch, and margin notes
at 26ch.

Layouts are CSS grid and fall into three shapes: content-plus-margin (`default`,
`image-right`), vertically centred single-idea (`statement`, `section`, `end`), and
title-canvas-foot (`diagram`, `options`). Anything that could overflow uses
`minmax(0, 1fr)` with `min-height: 0` so a long row cannot collide with the footer.

**The Reserved Space Rule.** Slides leave visible empty area on purpose, and the cover
says so out loud. The space is where live annotation goes, so filling it to balance a
composition defeats the point.

## Elevation & Depth

Effectively flat. There are no shadows inside a slide at all: depth is carried by the
sunk panel tone (`#eceff2`) for code and by wash fills behind rough boxes. The only
shadow in the system sits on the slide container itself, separating the canvas from the
letterbox on a cast screen.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Slide lift** (`box-shadow: 0 2px 18px rgb(25 28 32 / 8%), 0 12px 44px rgb(25 28 32 / 6%)`): the slide container only, and the single shadow in the system. Never reuse it on slide content.

### Named Rules

**The No Interior Shadow Rule.** Nothing inside the canvas is lifted. If an element needs
to separate from the surface, it gets a rough stroke or a wash, not a shadow.

## Shapes

The form language is the hand-drawn stroke, generated at render time by roughjs and
sized to its host box by a `ResizeObserver`.

House stroke style is `roughness: 1.15`, `bowing: 1`, `strokeWidth: 1.9`. Rounded boxes
override to `roughness: 0.72`, `bowing: 0.6`, because rounded corners overshoot at house
roughness and grow visible ears. Radii run 9px (chip), 11px (diagram node, choice), 14px
(default box), with CSS radii of 10px on code panels and images and 5px on inline code.

Strokes read their colour from CSS custom properties at paint time, so shapes follow the
active palette (including the high-contrast override) with no prop plumbing. A
`--rd-stroke-scale` multiplier thickens every stroke by 1.4 in high-contrast mode.

### Named Rules

**The Stable Seed Rule.** Every shape takes a deterministic seed derived from a string,
so its wobble is identical on every repaint. Re-randomising on render reads as a glitch,
not a sketch. Give each instance its own seed string.

**The Dot Grid Rule.** Exactly one layout (`board`) has a dot grid, where it marks the
slide as somewhere to draw and gives freehand strokes something to align to. It is
pinned to a light value in high-contrast mode so it does not swamp the drawings it exists
to support.

## Components

### Rough Box

- **Character:** a box someone drew around something to say "this part".
- **Shape:** rounded rect at 14px default, roughjs `roughness: 0.72` / `bowing: 0.6`, stroke weight 1.9 scaled by `--rd-stroke-scale`.
- **Color:** stroke from `--rd-pen-{pen}`; optional `fill` paints the matching wash at `fillStyle: solid`.
- **Padding:** 18px 22px, overridable via `--rd-box-pad`.
- **Distinctive:** the SVG is sized from the host box, and roughjs is given explicit `width`/`height`/`viewBox` attributes because it reads its generator size from those, not from CSS layout.

### Mark

- **Character:** the pen going back over text that already exists.
- **Variants:** `underline` (default), `circle`, `bracket`, `strike`.
- **Color:** stroke from `--rd-pen-{pen}`, weight 2.4.
- **Distinctive:** the circle variant carries 0.24em vertical padding and a negative inline margin, because the ellipse draws inside the element box and will otherwise cross the text and the line beneath it.

### Rough Arrow

- **Character:** a connector, not an ornament.
- **Shape:** quadratic curve with two head strokes, default box 56×34px.
- **Color:** stroke from `--rd-pen-{pen}-ink`, because an arrow carries meaning and has to survive the back of the room.
- **Distinctive:** `up` and `down` swap to a portrait box automatically; a vertical arrow in a landscape box draws as a stub.

### Note

- **Character:** something written in the margin during the meeting, not part of the slide.
- **Typography:** Shantell 500 at 19px, max 26ch.
- **Color:** `--rd-pen-{pen}-ink`. Never the stroke variant.
- **Distinctive:** optional leading arrow, which must point at the thing the note is about.

### Choice

- **Character:** one row of a comparison you are about to decide between.
- **Shape:** a 10.5rem name column beside a flowing body; the recommended row gains a filled Rough Box.
- **Distinctive:** rows separate by a rough rule and whitespace, never as equal-weight cards, so the recommendation can visibly outweigh the alternatives it beat. Set `pick` on exactly one.

### Diagram Node

- **Character:** a labelled box on a canvas, the artifact the whole system is named for.
- **Shape:** Rough Box at 11px radius, 10px 16px padding.
- **Typography:** Shantell 700 at 20px for the label, Recursive 14px for the sub-label.

### Code Panel

- **Character:** a printed excerpt set into the canvas, not an editor window.
- **Shape:** sunk `#eceff2` panel at 10px radius, 20px 32px padding, no chrome.
- **Distinctive:** a five-hue Shiki theme built from the same pen palette, so a code slide belongs to the deck. Focus is carried by the ink differential (focused lines at full ink, the rest at `--rd-code-dim`), with `--rd-code-focus` as a band that only locates the group. Dimming by colour rather than opacity keeps unfocused code at 4.7:1, where `opacity: 0.55` dropped it to 3.69:1.

### High-Contrast Mode

- **Character:** a delivery affordance for a bad room, not a second theme.
- **Behavior:** an `.rd-hc` class on the document root, toggled with `c`, persisted in `localStorage`. Pure black on pure white, strokes ×1.4, body and label type one step larger, every pen at its text-safe variant.
- **Distinctive:** it lives on the root rather than the slide container so overlays, the presenter view, and the drawing toolbar inherit it too.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** take `--rd-pen-{name}-ink` for anything that is text and `--rd-pen-{name}` for anything that is a stroke.
- **Do** give every rough shape its own stable `seed` string.
- **Do** add each new type step to the `.rd-hc` block at the same time as the light palette.
- **Do** let colour mean something (red problem, blue caveat, violet affirmative) and back it with position, weight, or a label.
- **Do** leave slides visibly unfinished, with room to draw in.
- **Do** reach for a diagram when a slide needs more presence.

### Don't:

- **Don't** add paper texture, sticky notes, tape, erase ghosting, or camera panning. All three skeuomorphic renditions were built and rejected by name.
- **Don't** put a shadow on anything inside the slide.
- **Don't** raise `RoughBox` roughness above 0.72; the corners grow ears.
- **Don't** use Shantell Sans for body copy or `em`, or switch typeface mid-sentence.
- **Don't** introduce a fourth typeface, a card grid, or a gradient.
- **Don't** put the dot grid on any layout but `board`.
- **Don't** carry a colour into the system without measuring it against its actual background.
